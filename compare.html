Competitive analysis · October 2026

Who we’re up against, and what we took from them.

Eleven products across medical, therapy, legal and advisory documentation. Every feature that showed up repeatedly in the market is now in the Verity prototype. Where we differ on purpose, the reason is stated.

1

The landscape

Medical · enterprise
AbridgeEnterprise

Epic-centred ambient scribe. Strongest evidence-linking story; dual output of clinical note plus patient summary; expanding into coding review.

Dragon Copilot (formerly DAX)Enterprise

Broadest enterprise EHR coverage (Epic, Oracle Health, Meditech) with Dragon dictation.

Medical · self-serve
NablaFree tier · ~$119/mo

Multilingual and telehealth-friendly; pre-charting, coding and decision support; many EHR integrations.

HeidiFree tier · paid plans

Customizable templates, voice commands, adapts to clinician style; builds documents from audio, PDF, Word and images.

Freed$39–$119/mo

Setup in minutes for solo and small clinics; browser-based EHR push; 14 languages.

Therapy
MentalycSubscription

Each note builds on prior sessions; drafts treatment plans with SMART goals, tracks progress, flags risk, notes per group member; deletes audio once the note is ready.

UphealFree tier · $39 flat

Live and post-session notes, built-in consent flows, session analytics such as talk ratios, telehealth and EHR in one tool.

Legal
Clio Manage AI (formerly Duo)In Clio plans

Practice-management AI: matter summaries, client emails, deadline extraction to calendar, time entries and billing narratives; tenant-isolated.

Financial advisors
Jump~$75–$100/advisor

Meeting prep and follow-up, CRM sync (Wealthbox, Redtail, Salesforce), customizable templates with summary verification; its bot joins the call.

Zocks~$67–$80/user

Never stores audio; extracts structured facts, fills forms, tracks consent, archives to compliance systems.

Where Verity is different on purpose

  • One product, many professions. Competitors are vertical. Verity shares the capture and evidence engine, with separate templates and safety rules per profession.
  • Five explicit claim states. Source-linked, verified, needs review, explicitly denied, not documented. Approval is blocked until flagged items are resolved.
  • Templates behave like software. Draft → test against a sample session → validation report → publish a version. Published changes never alter approved documents.
  • No clinical decision support. Nabla offers it; we deliberately don’t. Verity documents what was said and doesn’t suggest diagnoses or legal conclusions.
  • Apple-native. Liquid Glass Mac app with an iPhone companion and Handoff, instead of a browser tab.
6

Not built yet

Also tracked on our roadmap.

  • Financial advisor pack. Suitability language, risk-tolerance changes, beneficiary flags, and archiving to compliance systems (Jump/Zocks territory).
  • Group and couples notes. One note per member with separate access (Mentalyc has this).
  • Native EHR embedding. Abridge and Dragon Copilot live inside Epic; we push approved documents into it.
  • Contradiction finder. Compare an intake interview with a later deposition (Clio Work).
  • Revenue-cycle review. Pre-bill coding review is moving into scribes (Abridge).
